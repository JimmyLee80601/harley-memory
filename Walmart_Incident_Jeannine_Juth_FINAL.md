--- THE LEGAL STANDARD: LACK OF INTENT DESTROYS THEFT ---

Retail theft requires intent to permanently deprive the merchant of property. A scan-and-go application failure — where the customer scans via the merchant's own app, the app then presents an incomplete cart at checkout, and the customer pays exactly what the app presented — is the definition of no criminal intent. Where the app drops items, the customer never forms intent; the merchant's software created the discrepancy.

Settled precedent confirms this defense and the cost of ignoring it:

- Lesleigh Nurse v. Walmart (Semmes, Alabama): Shopping at self-checkout, the machine froze, Walmart accused her of shoplifting groceries, she was detained and a warrant issued. The charge was dropped. She sued for false arrest and malicious abuse of process, and a jury awarded her $2.1 MILLION in 2021, finding Walmart liable for "abuse of process" and a "pattern and practice of falsely accusing innocent citizens."
- Brianna Jones (Federal lawsuit, Charlotte, NC, filed May 2026): Accused of theft over a scanner error on a $5.01 item, she sued Walmart over wrongful detention and public humiliation, asserting Walmart hid its faulty self-checkout AI.

These are not isolated. One person a month contacting lawyers has reported being accused of retail theft from an underscan at Walmart's self-checkout / scan-and-go.
